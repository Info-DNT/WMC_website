When Should You See a GP for Fever?

Introduction

A fever is a temporary elevation in body temperature, often as part of the body's immune response to an infection. While many fevers resolve with rest and hydration, knowing when to consult a General Practitioner is crucial for timely care.

Key Information

A normal body temperature averages 37°C (98.6°F). A fever is typically defined as a temperature reading of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher. Fevers can be caused by viral infections, bacterial illnesses, or systemic inflammatory responses.

Symptoms & Considerations

  • Mild fever accompanied by body aches, mild sore throat, or tiredness.
  • High fever (above 38.5°C) persisting longer than 48–72 hours.
  • Associated signs such as chills, sweating, or mild dehydration.

When to Consult a Doctor

You should seek prompt medical consultation with a GP if your fever exceeds 39°C, persists beyond 3 days, or is accompanied by severe headache, stiff neck, shortness of breath, or rash.

Q: Can I take over-the-counter fever reducers before seeing a doctor?

A: Paracetamol or ibuprofen may help reduce comfort symptoms, but consulting a doctor is recommended if fever persists or causes concern.

Common Causes of Cough and Cold Symptoms

Introduction

Cough and cold symptoms are among the most frequent reasons individuals seek primary healthcare. Identifying whether symptoms are due to a simple viral cold, seasonal allergies, or a respiratory condition helps guide appropriate treatment.

Key Information

The common cold is usually caused by rhinoviruses affecting the upper respiratory tract. Coughing is a protective reflex that clears airway secretions and irritants.

Symptoms & Considerations

  • Runny or congested nose, sneezing, and sore throat.
  • Dry or productive cough lasting 1 to 2 weeks.
  • Distinguishing viral colds from seasonal allergic rhinitis.

When to Consult a Doctor

Consult a GP if your cough lasts more than 3 weeks, produces thick discolored mucus, causes wheezing, chest discomfort, or is accompanied by high fever.

Relevant WMC Service: GP Primary Care Clinic

Article FAQ

Q: Do common colds require antibiotics?

A: No, viral colds do not respond to antibiotics. A doctor will evaluate if secondary bacterial infections are present before prescribing medication.

Understanding Headaches

Introduction

Headaches are a very common symptom experienced by people of all ages. Understanding the different types of headaches helps determine lifestyle remedies and when a medical evaluation is required.

Key Information

Headaches are broadly classified into primary headaches (tension, migraine, cluster) and secondary headaches (caused by sinus infection, hypertension, stress, eye strain, or dehydration).

Symptoms & Considerations

  • Tension Headaches: Dull aching pain band around the forehead or back of head.
  • Migraines: Throbbing localized pain often with light sensitivity or nausea.
  • Sinus Headaches: Pressure around eyes, cheeks, and forehead accompanied by nasal congestion.

When to Consult a Doctor

Consult a physician if you experience sudden severe "thunderclap" headaches, headaches following head injury, or headaches with vision changes, numbness, or fever.

Relevant WMC Service: General Practitioner Consultations

Article FAQ

Q: How can dehydration cause headaches?

A: Dehydration causes blood vessels to constrict slightly, leading to pressure sensations. Drinking water often relieves mild dehydration headaches.

Understanding Blood Pressure

Introduction

High blood pressure (hypertension) is often called a "silent condition" because it may not produce noticeable symptoms until elevated for prolonged periods. Regular blood pressure tracking is essential for cardiovascular health.

Key Information

Blood pressure measures the force of blood against artery walls. A standard normal reading is around 120/80 mmHg. Consistent readings above 130/80 mmHg indicate stage 1 hypertension.

Symptoms & Considerations

  • Hypertension often causes no obvious early warning signs.
  • Severe hypertension may cause occasional headaches, dizziness, or shortness of breath.
  • Risk factors include high sodium intake, sedentary lifestyle, stress, and family history.

When to Consult a Doctor

Schedule a routine health check-up to measure blood pressure. If home readings consistently exceed 140/90 mmHg, consult a doctor for personalized evaluation.

Common Symptoms of Kidney Stones

Introduction

Kidney stones are hard mineral deposits that form inside the kidneys. Early recognition of kidney stone symptoms helps prevent severe discomfort and urinary complications.

Key Information

Stones develop when urine becomes concentrated with minerals such as calcium, oxalate, and uric acid. Dehydration is one of the primary risk factors for stone formation.

Symptoms & Considerations

  • Sharp, severe pain in the back, side, or lower abdomen (flank pain).
  • Pain or burning sensation during urination.
  • Pink, red, or brown discolored urine (hematuria).
  • Frequent urge to urinate in small amounts.

When to Consult a Doctor

Consult a Urologist immediately if flank pain is intense, accompanied by fever, chills, nausea, vomiting, or blood in the urine.

Q: Can drinking more water help prevent kidney stones?

A: Yes, adequate daily fluid intake dilutes urine and is the most effective natural way to reduce stone formation risk.

When Should You See a Urologist?

Introduction

A Urologist specializes in diagnosing and treating conditions affecting the urinary tract system in men and women, as well as the male reproductive system.

Key Information

Urological health encompasses the kidneys, ureters, bladder, urethra, and male reproductive organs. Specialized care helps manage urinary symptoms effectively.

Symptoms & Considerations

  • Persistent difficulty or pain when urinating.
  • Recurrent urinary tract infections (UTIs).
  • Urinary incontinence or weak urine flow.
  • Testicular discomfort or swelling in men.

When to Consult a Doctor

Schedule a urology consultation if you experience persistent urinary symptoms, blood in urine, or require routine prostate screenings after age 40.

Article FAQ

Q: Do urologists only treat male patients?

A: No, urologists treat urinary tract issues in both men and women, including bladder problems and kidney stones.

Understanding Prostate Health

Introduction

Prostate health is a fundamental aspect of men's wellness, particularly as men age. Understanding common prostate conditions promotes early detection and peace of mind.

Key Information

The prostate is a small gland located below the bladder. As men grow older, the prostate can undergo non-cancerous enlargement (Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia - BPH).

Symptoms & Considerations

  • Frequent urination, especially waking up multiple times at night (nocturia).
  • Difficulty starting urination or weak, interrupted urinary stream.
  • Sensation that the bladder is not completely empty.

When to Consult a Doctor

Men experiencing urinary changes or those over 40 years old should consult a Urologist for routine prostate evaluations and PSA blood testing.

A: PSA (Prostate-Specific Antigen) is a routine blood test used by urologists to evaluate prostate health status.

Understanding Child Growth Monitoring

Introduction

Routine growth monitoring allows pediatricians and parents to track physical, cognitive, and developmental milestones throughout infancy and childhood.

Key Information

Pediatric growth charts measure weight, height/length, and head circumference against WHO standardized percentiles to ensure healthy physical progress.

Symptoms & Considerations

  • Tracking steady growth velocity rather than single measurements.
  • Monitoring motor skill development, speech milestones, and social engagement.
  • Ensuring balanced nutrition and childhood vaccination schedules.

When to Consult a Doctor

Consult a Pediatrician if a child shows persistent growth faltering, significant milestone delays, or appetite changes.

Relevant WMC Service: Pediatric Clinic & Growth Monitoring Services

Article FAQ

Q: How often should infants have wellness growth checkups?

A: Routine visits are recommended at birth, 2, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, and 24 months, followed by annual checkups.

What to Know Before a Blood Test

Introduction

Diagnostic blood tests provide vital insights into metabolic health, organ function, and blood chemistry. Proper preparation ensures accurate laboratory test results.

Key Information

Certain tests (such as fasting blood glucose or lipid profiles) require 8 to 12 hours of fasting, while others require normal hydration.

Symptoms & Considerations

  • Fasting Guidelines: Drink only plain water during fasting periods.
  • Medication Disclosure: Inform lab staff of daily prescription medications or supplements.
  • Rest & Comfort: Staying relaxed and hydrated makes vein collection smoother.

When to Consult a Doctor

Follow your prescribing physician's instructions regarding lab preparations. Consult a GP to discuss your complete diagnostic blood report.

What to Expect During Your First Laser Hair Removal Consultation

Introduction

Laser hair removal is a light-based aesthetic procedure designed to reduce unwanted body hair growth. A initial consultation ensures treatment suitability and safety.

Key Information

The laser targets melanin in hair follicles. Multiple sessions are typically required because hair grows in distinct cycles (anagen, catagen, telogen).

Symptoms & Considerations

  • Pre-Treatment Care: Shave the treatment area 24 hours prior; avoid waxing or plucking.
  • Patch Testing: Small test pulses are applied to verify skin sensitivity.
  • Post-Treatment Care: Avoid direct sun exposure and apply soothing aloe gel.

When to Consult a Doctor

Schedule a consultation with an aesthetic medical practitioner to assess your skin phototype, hair density, and recommended session plan.

Relevant WMC Service: Laser Hair Removal Clinic Services

Article FAQ

Q: Is laser hair removal suitable for both men and women?

A: Yes, laser hair removal is commonly performed for both women and men across approved body areas.
